# The Clockmaker of Veyrath

In the forgotten village of Veyrath, hidden between the mountains of northern Eldoria, there lived an unusual clockmaker named Orin Valther. He was famous for creating clocks that did not measure hours or minutes, but memories.

Orin's workshop was located beneath an abandoned blue tower, near the eastern river called Nym. Every clock he created contained a small crystal known as a Chronite. These crystals were extracted from the caves of Mount Velkar, a mountain that villagers believed could hear human thoughts.

Orin had a daughter named Elira, who was twenty-three years old. Unlike her father, Elira was fascinated by astronomy. She owned a silver telescope called Asterion, which she had received from her mother before her disappearance.

One rainy evening, a mysterious traveler arrived at the workshop. His name was Kaelen Drav, and he carried a broken golden clock. The clock had three unusual symbols engraved on its back: a raven, a crescent moon, and a key.

Kaelen explained that the clock belonged to the ancient Kingdom of Thalmoor, which disappeared exactly 417 years earlier. According to an old legend, the clock could reveal the location of the kingdom, but only when it was repaired using a Chronite crystal taken from the deepest chamber of Mount Velkar.

Orin agreed to repair it, but he discovered something unexpected. The clock did not contain twelve mechanisms like ordinary clocks. Instead, it contained thirteen gears, and the smallest gear was made from an unknown black metal called Noctarium.

Elira recognized the metal from an astronomical manuscript she had found in her mother's belongings. The manuscript claimed that Noctarium appeared only during a celestial event called the Black Aurora, which occurred once every 89 years.

The next Black Aurora was predicted to happen in exactly eleven days.

On the seventh day, Orin, Elira, and Kaelen traveled to Mount Velkar. They entered the cave through an opening hidden behind a waterfall known as the Whispering Veil. After walking for six hours, they discovered a circular chamber containing a stone table and seven empty crystal holders.

At the center of the table was a message written in an ancient language:

"Time remembers what humanity forgets. The seventh crystal opens the door, but the first memory reveals the truth."

Orin noticed that six crystal holders were empty, while the seventh contained a small blue crystal. It was not Chronite. It was something much older, called Elarion.

When Elira touched the crystal, she experienced a memory of her mother standing inside the blue tower, speaking to someone whose face was hidden behind a silver mask.

The memory revealed that Elira's mother had not disappeared accidentally. She had voluntarily entered the lost Kingdom of Thalmoor to protect an object called the Heart of Hours.

The group returned to Veyrath just before the Black Aurora began. Orin repaired the golden clock using the Elarion crystal instead of Chronite.

At midnight, the thirteen gears started moving backward. The clock projected a map onto the workshop wall. It showed a hidden entrance beneath the eastern river Nym.

Kaelen revealed his true identity: he was the last surviving guardian of Thalmoor, and he had been searching for the Heart of Hours for twenty years.

Elira decided to follow him beneath the river, but Orin refused to let her go alone. Before they left, he gave her his most important creation, a small copper pocket watch called Memory's Last Light.

The watch had only one function: it could restore a single forgotten memory, but using it would permanently erase the user's happiest memory.

The three companions entered the underground passage beneath the Nym River. At its end, they found a massive silver door bearing the same three symbols from the golden clock: the raven, the crescent moon, and the key.

Elira placed the golden clock into a circular opening in the door.

The door opened.

Beyond it stood the lost Kingdom of Thalmoor, perfectly preserved beneath the earth. Its streets were illuminated by thousands of floating blue crystals.

But at the center of the kingdom, inside a glass chamber, stood Elira's mother.

She had not aged a single day in twenty-three years.

And beside her stood a second clockmaker.

It was Orin Valther.

The Orin standing next to Elira suddenly disappeared.

The truth was finally revealed: the Orin who had raised Elira was only a memory created by the Heart of Hours. The real Orin had sacrificed himself twenty-three years earlier to protect his daughter from the kingdom's final curse.

Elira now had to decide whether to use Memory's Last Light to bring her father back, knowing that she would lose every happy memory she had ever shared with him.

The clock stopped ticking.

And for the first time in 417 years, time itself waited for someone's decision.
